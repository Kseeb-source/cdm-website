Diagnosis

Why did my business stop appearing in AI answers?

AI assistants recommend businesses by name, and losing that spot feels sudden and unexplained. If ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity or Copilot used to mention you and now name a competitor, here is what actually happened and how to fix it.

The short answer

You dropped out because the evidence AI models trusted about you changed, not because you were penalised. The usual causes are a blocked crawler, a source page that stopped naming you, or a competitor with cleaner facts. Fix access, rebuild consistent facts, and most businesses are quoted again within weeks.

The most common reasons you dropped out

Your business dropped out because the evidence the models trusted about you changed, not because you did something wrong. AI assistants rebuild every answer from scratch when a question is asked. They name whoever has the clearest, most consistent proof at that moment. If a competitor published better matching pages, or your own source moved, you slipped below the line the assistant reads from and stopped being quoted.

Four crawlers do most of the collecting. GPTBot feeds ChatGPT, Google-Extended feeds AI Overviews and Gemini, PerplexityBot feeds Perplexity, and Bingbot feeds Microsoft Copilot. If any of them were blocked, timed out, or met a broken page during their last visit, your business quietly falls out of that assistant. The others may still name you, which is why owners often see the drop on one tool first and assume the whole web has forgotten them.

The page a model quoted may also have changed underneath you. A directory dropped your listing, a review site rewrote its ranking, or a news article aged and lost authority. Models lean on sources they already trust, so when a trusted page stops mentioning you, your citations fall with it. The fix starts with finding which source carried you, then working out whether it changed, disappeared, or was simply beaten by a fresher one.

  • Blocked crawler: a robots rule or firewall stopped one AI bot reading your site.
  • Lost source: the third party page that named you was edited or removed.
  • Fresher rival: a competitor published clearer, more specific proof.
  • Stale content: your own pages no longer match the question being asked.

How AI assistants decide who to name

Assistants do not rank you the way a search engine does. They assemble an answer from sources that agree with each other. When three or four pages describe your business the same way, the model treats that as fact and repeats it. When your details conflict across the web, the model hedges and names someone whose story is consistent. Agreement, not volume, is what gets you quoted, so scattered or contradictory information is a common silent cause.

Specifics win. A model would rather quote a page that says what you do, who you serve, and where, than a page of vague marketing language. If your site once carried plain facts and later replaced them with slogans, you gave the assistant less to hold onto. The same is true of dates. Content with a clear, recent timestamp reads as current, while undated pages look stale and get passed over for a rival that looks fresher.

Trust also travels through mentions on other sites. When respected pages in your field describe your business, assistants borrow that confidence. If those mentions dry up, get buried, or point to an old web address, the signal weakens. This is why a site redesign or a domain change can trigger a sudden drop. The links still exist, but they now lead nowhere useful, and the model has less reason to name you with confidence.

What probably changed on your side

Start by checking the plumbing, because self-inflicted drops are the most common and the easiest to reverse. A new theme, a security plugin, or a migration can add a rule that blocks AI crawlers without touching human visitors. Your site looks fine in a browser and still vanishes from answers. Check your robots file and server logs for GPTBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot and Bingbot, and confirm each one reached a working page recently.

Content edits are the next suspect. Rewriting a page for style, merging two pages, or deleting an old post can remove the exact sentence a model was quoting. Redirects that were set up wrongly send both people and bots to a page that no longer answers the question. If your drop lines up with a recent redesign or content clean-up, compare the old and new versions and look for facts that were lost in the process.

Speed and errors matter more than owners expect. If your pages load slowly, return errors, or depend heavily on scripts to show their text, a crawler may give up before it reads anything useful. It records an empty or broken visit and moves on. Over a few weeks, that thin record pushes you out of answers. A plain, fast page with the key facts in ordinary text is read far more reliably than a heavy one.

What changed on their side

Sometimes you did nothing and still dropped, because the assistants change constantly. Each provider updates its models and its sources on its own schedule, and a refresh can reshuffle who gets named overnight. A source they trusted last month may be weighted differently now. These shifts are rarely announced, so the first sign is your own missing name. This is normal, and it is recoverable, but it means you cannot set your evidence once and forget it.

Competitors also move. If a rival started publishing clear, factual, well structured pages, they may now be the cleanest match for the question you used to own. The model is not punishing you. It simply found a better source to quote. Watch which businesses now appear in the answers you have lost, read the pages they are being quoted from, and you will usually see exactly what kind of evidence the assistant currently prefers.

Answer formats change too. An assistant may start pulling shorter answers, or leaning on a different type of source, such as a directory over a blog. If your strongest content sat in the format they stopped favouring, your visibility falls even though the content is still good. The response is to spread your facts across more than one format, so a change in preference never removes every place a model can find you.

How to get named again

Recovery is methodical, not magical. You find the pages that used to carry you, fix what blocks the crawlers, and give the models clean, consistent facts to quote again. Most businesses see movement within a few weeks of a crawler regaining access, because the assistants re-read the web often. The goal is not to trick a model. It is to be the clearest, most consistent answer to the question your customers actually ask.

1

Find where you were quoted

Ask each assistant the questions you expect to win and note who it names and which pages it cites. That reveals the source you lost and the competitors who replaced you.

2

Unblock the crawlers

Check your robots file, firewall and server logs for GPTBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot and Bingbot. Confirm each one reached a working page recently.

3

Rebuild the facts

Put what you do, who you serve and where in plain text, worded the same way across every page so the models see one consistent story.

4

Refresh the source pages

Update directory listings and third party mentions that carried you, fix old web addresses, and give trusted sources current, accurate details to repeat.

5

Recheck on a schedule

Re-test the same questions every couple of weeks across all four assistants and keep a simple record of who gets named.

Do not judge success on a single check. Answers vary between people and shift from day to day, so test the same questions across ChatGPT, AI Overviews, Perplexity and Copilot over a couple of weeks before you conclude anything. Keep a simple record of who gets named each time. When your name returns on one assistant first, that is normal, and it usually means the others are following as their sources catch up.

Frequently asked
How long does it take to reappear in AI answers?
Usually a few weeks once the blocker is fixed, because AI crawlers re-read the web often. GPTBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot and Bingbot each visit on their own schedule, so one assistant may name you again before the others do. There is no fixed guarantee.
Did Google penalise my site?
Almost never. Dropping out of AI answers is not a penalty. It usually means a crawler was blocked, a source that named you changed, or a competitor published clearer evidence. Check your robots file and server logs first, then compare your pages against the ones now being quoted.
Why do I appear on ChatGPT but not Perplexity?
Because each assistant uses different crawlers and sources. ChatGPT relies on GPTBot and Perplexity on PerplexityBot, and they weigh evidence differently. If one bot was blocked or met a broken page, you fall out of that tool while the others still name you. Check each crawler separately.
Can a website redesign remove me from AI answers?
Yes. A redesign can block crawlers, break redirects, delete the exact facts a model quoted, or change your web address so trusted mentions lead nowhere. Compare the old and new pages, restore lost facts, and confirm each AI crawler still reaches a working version.
How do I find which page the AI was quoting?
Ask the assistant the question you used to win and look at the sources it cites. Perplexity and Copilot show links directly. For ChatGPT and AI Overviews, note the businesses now named and read the pages behind them to see the evidence being preferred.
