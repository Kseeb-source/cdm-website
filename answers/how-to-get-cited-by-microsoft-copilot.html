How do I get my business cited by Microsoft Copilot?

Microsoft Copilot answers millions of buying questions each day, and it names specific businesses when it does. If it never names yours, you lose deals before a prospect ever reaches your website. Here is how to change that.

The short answer

Copilot cites businesses it can find and trust. That means being indexed by Bing, publishing pages that answer the exact question a buyer asks, and earning mentions on sites Copilot already reads. Do all three and your name starts appearing inside answers rather than being left out of them.

Why Copilot names some businesses and not yours

Copilot cites you when it can find you, read you and trust you. It leans heavily on the Bing index and on live web results, then blends them with the underlying language model. If Bing has not crawled your site, or your pages never state a clear answer, Copilot has nothing to quote. The businesses it names are usually the ones that made themselves the easiest, clearest source to lift a sentence from.

This is different from ordinary search ranking. A blue link rewards the page that ranks first. A citation rewards the page that gives the cleanest, most quotable answer to a specific question. You can rank on page two of Bing and still be cited, and you can rank well yet be skipped because your page buries the answer under three paragraphs of throat clearing.

Copilot also cross-checks. It prefers claims that appear in more than one place, so a business described consistently across its own site, a directory listing and a review platform looks more reliable than one that exists only on its homepage. The goal is not to trick the model. It is to be the source a careful reader would pick.

Get indexed by Bing first

Bing indexing is the entry ticket, because Copilot cannot cite a page it has never seen. Start there before anything else. Most owners assume Google coverage means Bing coverage, and that assumption is often wrong. Bing crawls less aggressively than Google, so a site that is fine on Google can be thin or missing on Bing for weeks.

1

Open Bing Webmaster Tools

Create a free account, add your site and verify ownership. This is the direct line to how Microsoft sees you, and it is where Copilot signals begin.

2

Submit your sitemap

Give Bing your XML sitemap so its crawler, Bingbot, can find every page. Then use URL inspection to check which pages are actually indexed.

3

Fix what blocks the crawler

Check robots.txt is not blocking Bingbot, confirm pages return a 200 status, and remove noindex tags from anything you want cited.

Give it time. After you submit a sitemap, Bing can take from a few days to a few weeks to crawl and index new or changed pages. Check back weekly rather than daily. Once a page shows as indexed in Bing Webmaster Tools, it becomes eligible for Copilot to read and quote. Until then, no amount of on-page work will help, because the front door is still shut.

Write pages Copilot can lift a sentence from

Structure the page so the answer is impossible to miss. Put the buyer's question as the heading, then answer it in the first two lines. Copilot, like a busy reader, takes the clearest sentence near the top. If your answer sits in the sixth paragraph, it loses to a competitor who put theirs in the first. Lead with the conclusion, then add the detail underneath.

Use plain, factual sentences and real specifics. Name the service, the locations you cover, who it suits and what a client gets. Add an FAQ section written in the exact words people type, because question and answer pairs map neatly onto how Copilot retrieves and repeats information. Short declarative sentences travel better than long ones, since the model can quote them whole without editing.

Add structured data where it fits. Schema markup for your organisation, products, services and FAQs helps Bing understand what each page is about and which fields belong together. It does not guarantee a citation, but it removes guesswork for the crawler. Keep one clear answer per page rather than cramming five topics onto one URL, so each page has a single job Copilot can recognise.

Build mentions on sites Copilot already reads

Get named on sites beyond your own, because Copilot trusts corroboration. When several sources describe your business the same way, the model treats that description as reliable and is more willing to repeat it. A single self-description on your homepage carries far less weight than the same facts confirmed across the wider web that Bing already crawls and indexes.

  • Business directories: claim and complete your listings so your name, category and location are consistent everywhere.
  • Review platforms: an active profile gives Copilot real language from customers to draw on.
  • Industry and trade sites: a mention in a relevant roundup or association page signals you belong in that field.
  • Press and guest articles: being quoted as a source builds the pattern Copilot looks for.

Keep the facts identical across all of these. Your business name, the services you offer and the regions you serve should match word for word wherever they appear. Mismatched details make the model less certain, and uncertainty means it reaches for a competitor it can describe with confidence. Consistency is the quiet work that makes everything else you publish more likely to be believed and repeated.

Track whether it is working

Test Copilot directly and watch what it says. Open Copilot and ask the questions your buyers ask, in their words, such as who offers your service in your area or which provider suits a given need. Note whether you are named, which page it cites, and which competitors appear instead. Repeat the same prompts every few weeks so you can see movement rather than a single snapshot.

Watch Bing Webmaster Tools alongside those tests. It shows which pages are indexed, which search queries bring people to your site, and any crawl errors that are holding you back. Rising Bing impressions for your target questions usually come before Copilot starts naming you, so treat that data as an early sign that the groundwork is taking hold and worth continuing.

Be patient and consistent. Citations do not appear the day you publish. Indexing takes time, corroboration takes time, and the model refreshes what it knows on its own schedule. Give each change several weeks before you judge it, keep your facts identical everywhere, and keep answering real questions clearly. The businesses that get cited are the ones that stay the clearest source over months, not days.

Frequently asked
How is getting cited by Copilot different from ranking on Bing?
Ranking places your link in a list. Being cited means Copilot repeats your answer inside its response and names you as the source. You can rank modestly and still be cited if your page states a clear, quotable answer near the top that the model can lift whole.
Does my business need to be on Bing for Copilot to find it?
Yes. Copilot relies heavily on the Bing index, so a page Bing has not crawled cannot be cited. Set up Bing Webmaster Tools, submit your sitemap, and confirm your key pages show as indexed before expecting Copilot to name you in any answer.
How long does it take to get cited by Copilot?
Expect several weeks, not days. Bing can take from a few days to a few weeks to index new pages, and corroboration across other sites takes longer still. Keep your facts consistent, answer real questions clearly, and re-test Copilot every few weeks to track progress.
Does schema markup help Copilot cite my business?
It helps indirectly. Schema markup tells Bing what each page is about and which details belong together, which makes your content easier to understand and retrieve. It does not guarantee a citation, but it removes guesswork for the crawler and supports the clarity Copilot rewards.
Why does Copilot recommend my competitors instead of me?
Usually because they are easier to find, read and trust. Their pages answer the exact question near the top, they are indexed by Bing, and the same facts about them appear across several sites. Copilot repeats the source it can describe with the most confidence.
