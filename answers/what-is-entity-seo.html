Definition

What is entity SEO, and why does it matter more now?

AI assistants no longer hand out a page of links. They name a few businesses and move on. If they keep naming someone else, the problem is usually not your quality. It is how well machines understand who you are.

The short answer

Entity SEO is the work of making your business a clearly defined, well corroborated thing that search engines and AI models recognise and name. It matters more now because assistants give one answer, not ten links, and they name only the sources they can confidently identify. If your facts are consistent everywhere, you get quoted. If they conflict, you get skipped.

What entity SEO actually is

Entity SEO is the practice of making your business a clear, named thing that search engines and AI models can recognise, describe and connect to other things. An entity is a person, company, product or place with defined attributes. Traditional SEO chased keywords on a page. Entity SEO makes the machine understand who you are, what you do and who you serve, so it can name you with confidence.

Think of the difference between a string of text and a known fact. The words best accountant are a string. Acme Accounting, a firm serving small retailers in three countries, is a fact with attributes. Search systems store facts about entities in a graph, a web of linked things. When your business is a solid node in that graph, the system can retrieve you and repeat you accurately.

This shift is why the same question can return your competitor instead of you. If a model has richer, more consistent information about them, it treats them as the safer answer. It is not judging quality the way a human would. It is judging how well defined and how well corroborated each option is. Entity SEO closes that gap by making your details easy to find, easy to match and hard to get wrong.

Why it matters more now than it used to

It matters more now because answers have replaced lists. For years the search result was ten blue links, and a confident click could rescue a weak listing. Assistants like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot now give one composed answer and name a few sources. If you are not one of the named things, you are absent. There is no second page to scroll to and no link for the reader to notice.

These systems also work from a model of the world, not just a page they crawled seconds ago. They lean on what they can corroborate across many sources. A business that appears the same everywhere, same name, same services, same locations, becomes easy to trust. A business described three different ways looks like three uncertain guesses. The reward for consistency used to be modest. Now it decides whether you are quoted at all.

The stakes rose again because buyers now ask assistants for a shortlist before they ask a human. Someone types who should I use for this and takes the three names the assistant returns. That list forms before your sales team hears a word. If your entity is weak, you never reach the conversation. This is a change in when the decision happens, not just where, and it favours businesses that machines already understand.

How search engines and AI models build a picture of you

They assemble you from many signals and check them against each other. No single page makes you an entity. The picture is built from your own site, from directories, from mentions on other sites, and from structured data that spells out facts in a format machines read. When those signals agree, the system forms a confident profile. When they conflict, it hedges, and hedging usually means it names someone clearer instead.

  • Your own pages that state plainly what you do and who you serve.
  • Structured data, the Schema.org markup that labels your name, services and contact facts.
  • Consistent listings across directories and profiles, with matching details everywhere.
  • Mentions and citations on sites the model already trusts.
  • Clear relationships that link you to your products, people and places.

The crawlers doing this work now include names worth knowing. Googlebot still indexes the open web. GPTBot from OpenAI, PerplexityBot and others collect what large models read. Many respect the robots file, so blocking them by accident removes you from the answer entirely. Check what you allow. A single misconfigured line can hide your best pages from the exact systems you want quoting you, and you will never see the traffic you lost.

What to do about it

Start by fixing your facts, then help the machines read them, then earn corroboration elsewhere. The order matters. There is no point earning mentions that point at a confused profile. Get your own house consistent first, publish the facts in a format machines parse, and only then work on the wider web. Most businesses can make real progress in a few weeks, though trust in the graph builds over months, not days.

1

Fix your core facts

Write your name, services, locations served and contact details once, correctly, and make every page and profile match.

2

Add structured data

Mark up your organisation, people and services with Schema.org so machines read facts rather than guessing from prose.

3

Clean your listings

Align every directory and profile to the same details, and remove or correct stale entries that disagree.

4

Earn trusted mentions

Get named on sites the models already read, so your entity is corroborated by more than your own words.

Do not skip the boring step of checking access. Confirm your robots file allows the crawlers you want, and confirm your important pages return quickly and without errors. A model cannot quote a page it cannot fetch. Review your work each quarter, because your services change and the assistants update their sources on their own schedule. Small, steady corrections keep your profile clean far better than one large push and then silence.

How to tell if it is working

Test it the way a buyer would. Ask each assistant the questions your customers ask, in plain language, and see who gets named. Do this in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Copilot, because each draws on different sources and each can name a different set. Keep a simple record of the answers over time. Movement from absent, to mentioned, to recommended is the signal you are chasing.

Watch for the softer wins too. You may start being described more accurately before you are recommended more often. Correct attributes are progress, because a wrong fact repeated by a confident model does real damage. Look at whether the assistant states your services, your locations and your specialism correctly. If it does, the corroboration is landing. If it still muddles you with another firm, your facts are not yet clear enough.

Give it a realistic timeframe. Your own pages and markup can be read within days once crawlers revisit. Wider corroboration, the mentions and listings that make a model confident, take weeks to months to settle. Progress is rarely a straight line, because these systems refresh at their own pace. The businesses that win treat this as maintenance, not a one off project, and check their standing on a regular schedule.

Frequently asked
Is entity SEO different from normal SEO?
Yes, entity SEO focuses on making your business a recognised thing with defined attributes, rather than ranking a page for keywords. Traditional SEO targets search terms. Entity SEO targets understanding, so search engines and AI models can name you accurately and connect you to your services and locations.
Which AI assistants should I care about?
Focus on ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot, because these are where most buyers now ask for recommendations. Each draws on different sources, so each can name a different set of businesses. Test all four, since being strong in one does not guarantee the others.
How long does entity SEO take to work?
Your own pages and structured data can be read within days once crawlers revisit. Wider corroboration from listings and mentions takes weeks to months to settle. These systems refresh on their own schedule, so treat it as ongoing maintenance rather than a single project with a fixed finish date.
Can I do entity SEO myself?
Yes, much of it is disciplined tidying. Write your facts once, make every page and profile match, add Schema.org markup, and check that crawlers can reach your site. The harder part is earning trusted mentions elsewhere and keeping everything consistent over time, which is where many businesses ask for help.
What is a knowledge graph?
A knowledge graph is a web of linked facts about things, storing entities and the relationships between them. Search engines and AI models use it to retrieve and repeat information with confidence. When your business is a clear node in that graph, systems can name you and describe you correctly.
