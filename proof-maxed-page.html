Why does mould keep coming back even after you clean it?

Mould returns because surface cleaning alone does not address the root cause: excess moisture. Without identifying where that moisture is coming from, whether that is condensation, rising damp, a hidden water leak, or penetrating damp, mould spores will re-establish within weeks. A thorough professional mould inspection traces the problem back to its source so that the right remediation work can be carried out once and for all.

What are the most common causes of mould in London homes?

Condensation is the single most frequent cause, though it is far from the only one. London's older housing stock, which includes Victorian terraces, converted flats and basement apartments, is particularly vulnerable to a range of moisture issues.

  • Condensation: warm, humid air meeting cold walls or windows, especially in kitchens and bathrooms
  • Hidden water leaks: slow drips from pipework, radiators or underfloor heating that go unnoticed for months
  • Penetrating damp: moisture entering through external walls, roofs or poorly sealed windows
  • Rising damp: groundwater moving up through older masonry without a functioning damp-proof course
  • Plumbing leaks in adjoining flats: a common issue in purpose-built blocks and converted properties

Because London properties are so varied in age and construction, pinpointing the exact cause requires specialist diagnostic equipment rather than guesswork.

Hidden leaks can be detected using non-destructive methods that avoid unnecessary damage to floors, walls and ceilings. Is it safe to remove mould yourself?

Small patches of surface mould on non-porous materials such as tiles or glass can often be managed at home using appropriate cleaning products and good ventilation, but there are clear limits to DIY treatment. You should contact a professional if:

  • The affected area is larger than roughly one square metre
  • Mould is growing on plasterboard, timber, soft furnishings or other porous surfaces
  • Anyone in the property has respiratory conditions, allergies or a weakened immune system
  • The mould has returned repeatedly despite previous cleaning
  • You can smell mould but cannot see it, suggesting it may be growing behind walls or under floors
  • The property is a rented home where the landlord has a legal duty of care

In these situations, professional mould remediation ensures spores are safely contained and removed using appropriate methods and protective equipment.

Tip: Before booking any mould treatment, have the moisture source professionally investigated first. Treating mould without resolving the underlying damp issue is a short-term fix that will cost you more in the long run.

What does a professional mould remediation service involve?

Professional mould remediation goes far beyond wiping down surfaces with bleach. A structured remediation process typically includes the following stages:

  1. Inspection and assessment: identifying the extent of mould growth and the moisture source driving it
  2. Containment: isolating the affected area to prevent spores spreading to clean parts of the property during treatment
  3. Removal: safely removing mould-affected materials where necessary and treating remaining surfaces with appropriate antimicrobial solutions
  4. Structural drying: using specialist drying equipment to reduce moisture levels in walls, floors and structural elements before any reinstatement work takes place
  5. Verification: post-treatment moisture readings and inspection to confirm the area is safe and dry

Following water damage or significant damp ingress, structural drying is a critical step that many general contractors overlook, often leading to mould recurrence months later.

Frequently asked questions

How quickly can mould become a health risk?
Mould can begin to affect air quality within 24 to 48 hours of spores establishing on a damp surface, though the health impact varies depending on the species present and the vulnerability of the occupants. People with asthma, allergies, respiratory conditions or weakened immune systems are at greatest risk. Persistent exposure to mould spores has been linked to coughing, wheezing, eye irritation and skin reactions. If you suspect mould in your property, arranging a professional inspection promptly is strongly advisable.
Can I claim on my home insurance for mould damage?
Whether mould damage is covered by home insurance depends on the cause. Mould resulting from a sudden, accidental water leak, such as a burst pipe, is often covered, while mould caused by long-term neglect or condensation typically is not. Damp and Mould Solutions UK provides detailed written reports suitable for insurance claims, which can help you demonstrate the cause and extent of the damage to your insurer.
How long does a damp or mould investigation take?
A typical damp or mould investigation for a residential property takes between one and three hours on site, depending on the size of the property and the complexity of the problem. Following the inspection, a written report is produced detailing findings, the probable cause and recommended next steps. Structural drying, where required, is a longer process that can take several days to a few weeks depending on the level of moisture saturation in the building fabric.
What is the difference between a mould inspection and mould remediation?
A mould inspection is a diagnostic survey carried out to assess the presence, extent and likely cause of mould growth using specialist moisture meters, thermal imaging and visual examination. Mould remediation is the treatment process that follows, involving the safe removal of mould and the drying and treatment of affected surfaces. An inspection should always come first so that remediation work is targeted correctly and any underlying damp or leak issues are identified before treatment begins.
